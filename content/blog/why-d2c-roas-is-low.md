"Our ROAS is terrible. Should we change agencies? Try Google instead? Stop ads completely?"

We hear this from D2C founders every week. In most cases, the ads aren't the only problem. Return on ad spend is the *result* of your whole funnel: tracking, creative, offer, website and product economics. Fix only the ads and ROAS usually stays stuck.

Here are the seven reasons we see most often, roughly in the order you should check them.

## 1. Your tracking is broken

If the platform cannot see your sales, it cannot optimise for buyers, and your reported ROAS will look worse than reality.

**Signs:** orders in Shopify are much higher than purchases reported in Ads Manager, or purchase events are duplicated.

**Fix:**

- Confirm the Meta Pixel and Conversions API are both active and deduplicated
- Check Google Ads conversion tracking and enhanced conversions
- Place a test order after any theme, app or checkout change
- Compare platform-reported purchases against real orders every week

## 2. You don't know your break-even ROAS

Many brands chase a ROAS of "4x" because someone told them it was good. The number that matters is **your** break-even point.

**Fix:** calculate it.

> Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ (gross margin after product cost, shipping, payment fees and expected returns)

If your true margin is 40%, your break-even ROAS is 2.5. Anything above that is profit, before fixed costs. Include **RTO on COD orders**: for many Indian brands this alone moves the number significantly.

## 3. Your creatives are tired (or never persuaded anyone)

Creative is the biggest lever in modern performance marketing. Two common patterns:

- **Fatigue:** a winning ad has run for weeks, frequency is climbing and click-through rate is falling
- **Weak angles:** ads look beautiful but say nothing that makes people want to buy

**Fix:** build a creative testing system. Launch new creatives weekly, test different *angles* (problem, occasion, social proof, offer, founder story), and judge them on thumb-stop rate and click-through rate before cost per purchase. Most brands need far more creative volume than they think.

## 4. Your audience setup is fighting the algorithm

Heavily segmented accounts with many small ad sets, narrow interests and overlapping audiences starve each ad set of data. Nothing exits the learning phase and costs stay high.

**Fix:** consolidate. Fewer campaigns, broader targeting, larger budgets per ad set. Let strong creatives find the right buyers.

## 5. Your website is leaking buyers

If people click and add to cart but don't buy, no amount of ad optimisation will help.

**Check these first:**

- **Mobile page speed.** Most paid traffic is on phones. Heavy images and apps slow stores down
- **Product pages.** Clear photos, real people using the product, size and fit guidance, reviews
- **Trust.** Return policy, delivery timelines and contact details visible before checkout
- **Checkout friction.** Surprise shipping costs, forced account creation, limited payment options
- **Message match.** The landing page should continue the promise the ad made

A small improvement in conversion rate raises ROAS on every rupee you spend, without touching the ads.

## 6. Your offer isn't compelling enough

Sometimes the product is good, the ads are good, and the offer still isn't strong enough to make a stranger buy from a brand they have never heard of.

**Fix:** test offers deliberately. Bundles, free shipping thresholds, a first-order incentive, gift-with-purchase or a strong guarantee often beat blanket discounts and protect margins better.

## 7. You're scaling too fast, or at the wrong time

Doubling budgets overnight often resets learning and spikes costs. And during peak seasons like Diwali, the cost of reaching people rises for everyone.

**Fix:** increase budgets gradually (around 15–20% every few days), add proven creatives as you scale, and plan festive campaigns weeks in advance with warm audiences and a stocked creative bank.

## A quick diagnostic checklist

Work through this in order. Stop at the first "no" and fix it before moving on:

1. Do platform-reported purchases roughly match real orders?
2. Do you know your break-even ROAS, including returns and RTO?
3. Have you launched new creatives in the last two weeks?
4. Is your account consolidated into a few well-funded campaigns?
5. Is your mobile conversion rate healthy and your checkout friction-free?
6. Is your offer strong enough for a first-time buyer?
7. Are you scaling budgets gradually?

## Don't forget retention

ROAS measures only the first order. A customer who buys three times a year is worth far more than one who buys once. **Email, WhatsApp flows and a good post-purchase experience** mean you can afford a lower first-order ROAS and still grow profitably. That is the difference between brands that scale and brands that stall.
