Fashion is one of the most competitive categories on Meta in India. Thousands of brands are chasing the same shoppers, costs rise every festive season, and a creative that worked last month can stop working overnight.

The good news: the brands that win are rarely the ones with the biggest budgets. They are the ones with **clean tracking, a simple account structure and a steady supply of creatives that actually persuade**. This playbook walks through exactly how to set that up.

> **The short version:** fix tracking first, keep your campaigns simple, spend most of your energy on creative testing, and only scale what has already proven it can sell.

## Step 1: Fix your tracking before you spend a rupee

Meta's delivery system learns from the conversion data you send it. If that data is incomplete, the algorithm optimises for the wrong people.

Before launching, make sure you have:

- **Meta Pixel installed** on every page of your store, including checkout and the thank-you page
- **Conversions API (CAPI)** connected. On Shopify this takes a few clicks through the Facebook & Instagram channel. It sends purchase data server-side so fewer sales go unreported
- **Standard events firing correctly:** ViewContent, AddToCart, InitiateCheckout and Purchase, with the correct order value and currency (INR)
- **Your domain verified** in Business Manager

Use Events Manager's Test Events tool to place a test order and confirm each event arrives once, not twice.

## Step 2: Know your numbers

Most fashion brands that "can't make Meta work" never decided what a good result looks like. Work out three numbers first:

| Number | What it means | How to calculate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Average order value (AOV) | What a typical customer spends per order | Revenue ÷ orders |
| Break-even ROAS | The ROAS where you neither make nor lose money | 1 ÷ gross margin after shipping, returns and payment fees |
| Target CAC | The most you can pay to acquire a customer | AOV × margin, adjusted for repeat purchases |

For fashion in India, **returns and RTO (return to origin) on COD orders** can quietly destroy your margin. Include them in your break-even calculation, or your "profitable" ROAS will not be profitable at all.

## Step 3: Keep the account structure simple

Complicated accounts with dozens of tiny ad sets split your budget so thinly that nothing exits the learning phase. A simple structure works better for most brands:

1. **Testing campaign.** One campaign where new creatives and angles are tested with a controlled budget.
2. **Scaling campaign.** An Advantage+ sales campaign (or a broad-targeting sales campaign) that only receives creatives which have already proven themselves in testing.
3. **Retargeting.** Optional once you have enough traffic. Show collection carousels, reviews and offers to people who viewed products or added to cart.

### Targeting in 2026: go broad

Detailed interest targeting matters far less than it used to. For most fashion brands, **broad targeting with the right age, gender and location** outperforms stacks of interests, because the creative itself does the targeting. A saree ad will find saree buyers; a streetwear ad will find streetwear buyers.

## Step 4: Treat creative as your real targeting

This is where fashion brands win or lose. The same product can fail with one ad and sell out with another.

Test **different angles**, not just different colours of the same ad:

- **Problem angle:** "Kurtas that don't fade after three washes"
- **Occasion angle:** "What to wear to a sangeet when you hate heavy lehengas"
- **Social proof angle:** real customers trying on the product, reviews on screen
- **Founder or brand-story angle:** why the brand exists, how it is made
- **Offer angle:** launch discount, free shipping, bundles

### Formats that work for fashion

- **Try-on videos** and "outfit of the day" styles filmed on a phone
- **Styling videos:** "3 ways to wear this one shirt"
- **UGC-style reviews** from real customers or creators
- **Before and after** for fit or shapewear categories
- **Carousels** of a full collection for retargeting

The **first two seconds** decide whether anyone watches. Open with movement, a person, a bold statement or a question, never with a logo.

## Step 5: Test with discipline

A simple testing rhythm:

1. Launch 3–5 new creatives per week in the testing campaign.
2. Give each enough spend to judge it. A useful rule of thumb is 2–3× your target cost per purchase before killing it.
3. Judge in this order: **thumb-stop rate (3-second views) → click-through rate → cost per add-to-cart → cost per purchase**.
4. Move clear winners into the scaling campaign. Turn off the rest.
5. Write down *why* you think each winner worked, and brief the next round of creatives from those learnings.

## Step 6: Scale without breaking what works

When a campaign is profitable, increase budget gradually, roughly **15–20% every few days**, rather than doubling overnight, which can reset learning and spike costs.

Other ways to scale:

- Add more winning creatives to the scaling campaign
- Expand to new product categories with their own proven creatives
- Plan ahead for **festive season** (Navratri, Diwali, wedding season). Costs rise sharply, so build your creative bank and warm audiences weeks before

## Step 7: Fix the website, not just the ads

Ads bring people to your store. The store has to close the sale. If your cost per add-to-cart looks healthy but purchases are low, the problem is usually on-site:

- Slow mobile pages (most Meta traffic is mobile)
- Unclear size charts, which also drive returns
- No reviews or real photos of people wearing the product
- A surprise shipping fee at checkout
- Too few payment options, or no COD for first-time buyers

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Boosting posts** instead of running proper sales campaigns
- **Judging ads after one day.** Give them enough spend
- **Changing too many things at once**, so you never learn what worked
- **Running the same creative for months** until performance collapses
- **Ignoring returns and RTO** when calculating ROAS

## The bottom line

Profitable Meta ads for fashion come down to a repeatable system: accurate tracking, clear numbers, a simple account, relentless creative testing and a website that converts. Get those right and budget becomes a lever you can pull with confidence rather than a gamble.
